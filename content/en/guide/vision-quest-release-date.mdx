## What VisionQuest Is About: Story, Mindspace, and Themes

The premise picks up directly from the end of *WandaVision*. After being resurrected as the all-white "White Vision" and then having his memories restored, Vision is a synthezoid with a complete database of a life he has no emotional connection to. Showrunner Terry Matalas has described him as a blank slate.

By the time the series opens, Vision has spent years hiding in plain sight, disguised as a human and working in a pub. When a bounty is placed on his head, he goes on the run with a mysterious teenager who may be his reincarnated son.

### Inside the Mindspace

To cope with his identity crisis, Vision retreats into an internal "Mindspace," where the AI personas embedded in his programming appear as human avatars. The production team leaned into a deliberately uncanny look for these sequences — stair banisters that lead nowhere, chairs built into walls, and subtly wrong furniture that mimics the glitches of AI-generated imagery.

The Mindspace mansion was a practical set that took 14 weeks to build, with seven fully connected rooms that allowed continuous camera movement. The kitchen alone featured more than 40 custom-branded props, including items like Firewall Hot Sauce and Mindstone Soup.

### Themes: Fatherhood and Identity

The *WandaVision* trilogy has always been about family, and *VisionQuest* pushes that theme into fatherhood specifically. Marvel's Brad Winderbaum framed the show as a three-generation story — grandfather, father, and son — asking whether a man raised by an abusive father can break the cycle. Bettany has said the series deals with denial of pain and coming to terms with who you are.

Each episode is also styled like a different genre of film, echoing the way *WandaVision* cycled through sitcom eras. Expect adventure, mystery, and a healthy dose of surreal comedy.
